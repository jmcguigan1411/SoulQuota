## What the range says
- $4.99 to $6.66: 2D games from one or two developers, sold on volume.
- $9.99: the one 3D game in the set. Its most-cited praise is value for money.
- $12.99: the two games with either online co-op as the headline or a known brand, both after long Early Access periods with a lot of content.
- Soul Quota is 3D with online co-op, which are the two features that sit at the top of this range. Its content today (3 maps, 21 reapers, 7 bosses, unproven polish) is closer to a launch-day Megabonk than to a 1.0 Spell Brigade.

## Steam rules that matter (Steamworks discount documentation)
- Launch discount: 10% to 40%, for 7 to 14 days, and it must be set up before release.
- No other discount for 30 days after release, and none within 30 days of another discount (seasonal sales are exempt).
- A base price increase cannot happen within 30 days of release, and it blocks any discount for 30 days afterwards. Price decreases have no cooldown.
- So: it is easy to lower a price later and awkward to raise one. Raising at the move from Early Access to 1.0 is the accepted moment.

## Early Access or full release
- Early Access fits where the game is: untested build, three maps, co-op that needs real players to tune. It also gives a natural point for one price rise.
- Risk: Early Access reviews are permanent. Launching before the game is fun and stable costs more than launching a few months later.

## Regional pricing
- Use Valve's suggested regional prices as the starting point and do not hand-set them lower; the suggestions already account for purchasing power. Review them at each price change.

## Recommendation
1. Early Access at $7.99 with a 10% launch discount for the first week. That undercuts Megabonk while offering co-op, which is the comparison buyers will make.
2. Raise to $9.99 at 1.0, once there are more maps and the co-op is proven. Announce the rise in advance so Early Access buyers feel rewarded.
3. Do not go to $12.99 unless the game has content and review scores to match the two games at that price.
4. Co-op games sell in groups: plan a 4-pack style bundle or frequent small discounts around friend-invite moments once Steam invites exist.

## What would change this
- If playtests show the game is thin or rough, $5.99 to $6.99 in Early Access is safer.
- If a publisher or a strong demo wishlist count appears, $9.99 from day one is defensible.
